# Three lyrics × fourteen styles — 42 full songs

`music/lyric-versions/`, named `<style>__<lyric>.mp3`. All 62s, all tail-checked, all
levelled to −16 LUFS. Rebuild any of them with
`python3 lyric_versions.py CT03_klezmer` on the box.

The three lyrics are **different songs, not rewrites of each other**. All three sing the
cafe's name — a jingle that doesn't isn't a jingle — but past that they argue for the place
in three different ways. Every image in them comes from something a real customer wrote in
`research/reviews/`.

---

## A · "The Chant" — `__A_chant`

Hook-first. Name shouted, short lines, built to stick. The one you've been hearing.

> Down on Store Street, second door, / green as green and gold on the sign — /
> there's a pickle on the wall who's been dancing since before / you or me or anyone in line.
>
> **Sour Pickle! Sour Pickle Cafe!** / Ain't a sour thing about it, no way. /
> Big plate, warm plate, come as you are — / the little cafe that'll make your day.
>
> She grew the lettuce in her own back yard, / he's been cracking the eggs since dawn. /
> Nothing here is fast and nothing here is hard, / just good and hot and gone.

## B · "Come Hungry" — `__B_hungry`

The food one. Portions, the griddle, the bottomless cup, the wait. Its hook is a promise
rather than a name-chant, which makes it the most *usable* as an end line.

> Three eggs folded over, cheese and green onion, / potatoes off the griddle, gold and hot, /
> toast cut on the corner, jam in a paper packet — / honey, that is not a little, that's a lot.
>
> **Come hungry, leave happy** / down at the Sour Pickle Cafe. / The cup never empties, the
> plate never quits, / and nobody's rushing you away.
>
> Ask her for the pickle, she'll cut the thing in half, / it's bigger than your hand, I swear
> it's true. / Everything's from scratch so give it twenty minutes — / that's twenty minutes
> somebody spent on you.

*("Ask for a sour pickle on the side — it's huge, you can split" — Julie S., Yelp. "We waited
20-25 minutes, but it was worth it" — Corbin Randel, Google.)*

## C · "Same Good Morning" — `__C_morning`

The story one. Twenty years, the regulars, the garden, the weather. Slowest-burning and the
only one that would carry a 60-second film rather than a title card.

> Twenty some years on the very same corner, / same green door, same eight o'clock. /
> There's a fella comes in every single morning / and she pours it out before he's got his
> coat off.
>
> **It's the same good morning** / at the Sour Pickle Cafe. / From a garden out back to a
> plate on the table — / some things are worth keeping that way.
>
> Blossom in the springtime falling on the awning, / rain in the winter down the glass. /
> Everybody's welcome, half of them are regulars, / and the other half are coming back.

*("I asked an old gentleman coming out how it was, and he said he had been eating breakfast
there for 20 years" — DLCAlberta, TripAdvisor.)*

## How the lyrics sit on the styles

Not every pairing works, and the mismatches are informative:

- **A on the fast cartoon styles** (bigband chase, klezmer, bassoon romp) is the title
  sequence. The chant needs tempo to land.
- **B on the mid-tempo swing** (organ swing, gypsy jazz, ragtime) is the best *radio* song
  of the 42 — it's the one with an actual argument in it.
- **C on the slow jazz** (torch, cool jazz, jazz trio, bossa) is the one that could score a
  60-second film. On a fast style it sounds like it's in a hurry to tell you something
  tender, which is the wrong shape.
