# Gallery

`/gallery`

## What this page is for

Every generation this project has made — 1828 of them — each carrying the exact recipe
that produced it. Not a mood board. A record you can read backwards.

Each entry holds: the seed, the workflow it used, the variables it set, what it was made
to demonstrate, and any warning attached to it.

## What to do first

Find a picture you like. Then read the recipe under it. Then use the filters at the top —
**every variable**, **every value**, **every template** — to find the other renders that
differ from it in exactly one field.

That is the whole method of this project: change one thing, look at both, and only then
say what the thing does. The gallery is where you do it.

## The three things that will confuse you

**1. The filters are the interface, not the scroll bar.**

1828 tiles is not browsable. The three selects narrow by variable, by value and by
template, and the search box searches prompts. Start with a variable, then pick a value,
and the grid becomes a comparison instead of a pile.

**2. Some entries have no name and no recipe.**

Five cells render nameless because their `demonstrates` field is a pair of dashes:
`voice__1785778085`, `voice__1785776842`, `music__1785776964`, `music__1785776821`,
`sfx__1785776970`. They are real generations with lost provenance. Ignore them; they are
not telling you anything.

**3. Most entries carry no `domain`.**

1780 of 1828 have the field empty. This does not affect the gallery itself, but it is why
`/make`'s audition strip looks so thin — that strip can only see the 48 entries that
have one.

## The `hide flagged` checkbox

Some entries carry a `warning`. The checkbox hides them. Leave it **off** while you are
learning. A flagged render is usually the most informative thing on the page: it is a
result somebody looked at and rejected, with a note saying why.

## What good output looks like here

Not a judgement of the pictures — a judgement of the **record**.

A gallery entry is good when you could reproduce it. That means:

- a seed,
- a named workflow,
- the variables it set,
- and a `demonstrates` field that names the one thing it was varying.

If a picture you like has all four, you can make another one like it on purpose. If it
does not, you got lucky and you cannot repeat it.
