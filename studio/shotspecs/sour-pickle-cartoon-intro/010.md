# Shot 010 — The drive-by brake

## WHAT HAPPENS

A camera held out of a car window, pointed 90 degrees left, travelling down Store Street and stopped dead by a hard brake outside the cafe.

## BUILT WITH

camrig drive_by_brake (studio/_tools/camrig.py) · plates2/street_x4_hero.png · build/grade_street.py then build/dress_shop.py

## MUST NEVER CHANGE

*These are the promises. I do not change one without telling you first.*

### Camera never rotates
The camera stays square to the facade for the whole shot. Vertical edges - piers, doorframes, downpipes - must stay vertical.
WHY: A pan rotates about the camera and keeps perspective fixed; driving past changes the viewing angle. Cross-dissolving an oblique plate into a square-on one IS a rotation, and it read as the camera turning to face the next building.
CHECK: camrig preset is sour_pickle

### Fronto parallel plate
The plate must be one fronto-parallel photograph containing BOTH buildings already square on. fb-08-latest-post.jpg, upscaled. Now street_x4_open.png - the same photograph with the cafe's real open frontage inserted into the arch by door_insert.py, which changes what is INSIDE the archway and nothing about the geometry.
WHY: Only then is a pure horizontal translation geometrically correct. Any other plate can only ever produce a pan, however fast it moves.
CHECK: anchor contains street_x4

### Three phase brake
Constant speed, then the framing ACCELERATES through the brake, then the return from full lean is faster still.
WHY: Phase 2 collapsing early reads as a car accident. camrig's verdict tests it.
CHECK: engine is cam

### Both buildings whole
Starts square on a complete neighbouring bay, ends square on the cafe bay - piers either side, arch above, shopfront below.
WHY: Starting at the plate's left edge cut INSIDE OUT in half; it read as the corner of something rather than a building.
