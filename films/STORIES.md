# Three sample stories

Written for the k4shix pipeline (Qwen keyframes → LTX-2.3 i2v → Chatterbox → ACE-Step).
All three are **original stories in the lineage of** the games named — same genre grammar,
same feeling, none of their characters, names or plot. That's a deliberate craft choice as
much as a legal one: a homage that has its own twist lands harder than a retelling of a
story the audience already knows the ending of.

## The constraint that shaped all three

The pipeline's weakest point is **human faces across many shots** — Qwen re-invents a face
every keyframe, and a film is 20 keyframes. The two films already on this box solved it by
casting automatons. So each story below casts a protagonist whose design *cannot* drift:

| Story | Hero | Why the design holds |
|---|---|---|
| A | helmeted crusader, visor never raised | a helm is a fixed silhouette — no face to drift |
| B | a girl seen from behind / in silhouette | the back of a coat is trivially consistent |
| C | a suit of empty armour, animated | no face exists to be inconsistent |

A faceless armoured hero is also the strongest *projection* surface there is — Doomguy,
Samus, Vader. The audience wears the character instead of watching it.

# A — THE HOLLOW CHOIR
### lineage: Diablo · gothic dark fantasy, descent, dread → awe

**Logline.** Forty years after the last sunrise, a lone crusader descends beneath a drowned
cathedral to silence the choir that has been singing without pause since the dark fell — and
learns the singing is the only thing keeping the dark asleep.

**Why it works as a short.** A descent is the most economical structure in film: every shot
is automatically *further in*, so a sequence of disconnected 4-second clips reads as
inevitable progress instead of a slideshow. And the twist inverts the hero's goal at the
midpoint, which is how you get a real story into 80 seconds instead of a mood reel.

**The turn.** She comes to stop the singing. She finds the singers are the townspeople —
forty years at it, throats ruined, unable to stop. Stopping them is what wakes the thing
below. So she does the harder thing: she takes up the song herself, and rings the great bell
the choir was built to replace. The bell does in one strike what forty years of singing
could only postpone.

**Ending.** First dawn in forty years, coming in through a rose window. She's still standing
there when the light hits. That's the inspiring beat — not victory, *relief*.

**Cast.** THE CRUSADER (helmed, never speaks more than eight words). THE CHOIRMASTER (a
spindly thing of pipe-organ parts and hymn-book pages, courteous, exhausted).

**Shape.** 21 shots · ~82 s · three music cues (dread → assault → hymn)

# B — THE LAST GOOD YEAR
### lineage: Chrono Trigger · time travel, bright past / dead future, hope

**Logline.** A girl finds a pocket watch that shows two times at once — and discovers the
bright summer town she lives in is one year away from ending. She has that year, and only
that year, to move one small thing.

**Why it works as a short.** The strongest cold open available to this pipeline is a
**hard cut between two versions of one image** — a sunlit town square, then the same square
as ash, same camera, same framing. It costs two keyframes and buys the entire premise with
no dialogue. That's the hook.

**The turn.** She tries the obvious big fixes across the year and they all fail — the future
reasserts itself. What finally works is absurdly small: she plants a tree in the square.
In the ruined future the tree is the one thing still standing, and it is holding up the
wall that everyone is sheltering behind.

**Ending.** Ruined future, but there are *people* in it now, under the tree. Same square,
third version. The rhyme of three identical framings is the whole film.

**Cast.** MERO (girl, dark coat, seen from behind or in silhouette — a red scarf is her
readable signature at any distance). THE WATCH (a voice; ticking, patient, slightly sad).

**Shape.** 18 shots · ~74 s · three cues (summer → unravelling → bittersweet)

# C — ASHFALL
### lineage: Dark Souls / Elden Ring · empty armour, futile loop, defiance

**Logline.** A suit of armour with nobody inside has climbed the same stair to the same
throne ten thousand times, lost ten thousand times, and starts up it again — and this run,
for the first time, it does something different.

**Why it works as a short.** A loop justifies **deliberate repetition**, which is a gift
when your shots are 4 seconds long: the same three framings recur with small escalating
differences, and repetition-with-variation is free tension. The whole film is a montage by
design rather than by limitation.

**The turn.** Every previous run it drew the sword. This run it drops the sword on the step
and keeps climbing empty-handed — and the thing on the throne, which has only ever known how
to answer a sword, has no answer for that.

**Ending.** It sits down on the throne's step beside the king. Two ruined things resting.
Ash stops falling. The most understated of the three, and the one most likely to be
mistaken for a real game trailer.

**Cast.** THE HOLLOW (empty plate armour, one wisp of blue light in the visor slot). THE ASH
KING (a mountain of a figure fused into the throne, crowned, asleep).

**Shape.** 16 shots · ~66 s · two cues (grinding dread → still and warm)

## Recommendation

**A — THE HOLLOW CHOIR**, for four concrete reasons:

1. **It shows off the box best.** Cathedral interiors, candle-light, ash, stained glass and
   hellfire are exactly what Qwen is strongest at, and high-contrast low-key light hides the
   softness in LTX's motion.
2. **The audio pipeline is the star.** A film whose *plot is a choir* justifies ACE-Step
   carrying it. The score isn't decoration here, it's the antagonist.
3. **It has the best hook** — a match struck in total blackness revealing a ceiling of
   hanging bells, before a single word.
4. **Grim → radiant** is the emotional arc that was asked for. C is the most tasteful but
   ends quiet; B is the cleverest but its power is in the framing rhyme, which needs the
   viewer's patience.

B is the best second render — it reuses nothing, so it's a genuinely different-looking film.
